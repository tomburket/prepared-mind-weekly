01Tariff Watch
Tariff
Volpe: tariff pass-through hits 4–4.5% by year-end as input costs converge
FoodNavigator-USA · Elizabeth Crawford · May 15, 2026
Ricky Volpe of California Polytechnic State University, a former USDA economist, projects grocery prices will close 2026 4 to 4.5% above the previous year as tariff effects converge with energy, packaging, and labor pressures. Tomatoes are now up 39.7% year over year per the most recent CPI, climbing 15.1% from March to April on top of a 15.3% increase from February to March. Packaging cost pressure from steel and aluminum tariffs is starting to surface and is expected to steepen.
Why it matters
The "tariff talk was much ado about nothing" reading does not survive contact with the actual category data. The pressure is converging — not just on imported produce but on cans, packaging, and the energy-intensive moves that get fresh product from field to shelf. Year-end is where the full pass-through will sit.

CEO Market
Walmart wins the tariff year while peers stall — and the gap is data, not just scale
Reuters via BNN Bloomberg · May 19, 2026
In the fiscal year ending Jan. 31, Walmart's top-line sales grew 4.7% while Target's fell 1.7%, Kroger stayed about flat, and Albertsons grew about 3.5%. Walmart shares are up roughly 50% since President Trump imposed tariffs in April 2025 — gains that "far eclipse competitors." Operating margin held nearly flat at 4.2%. Walmart's high-margin advertising and membership-fee businesses combined for an estimated 27% of operating profits last fiscal year, up from 9% in 2021, per Morningstar data.
Why it matters
Morningstar analyst Brett Husslein on the dynamic: "When the economy is hurting or people feel like their wallet is stretched, they go to Walmart." The harder question is whether the gap is replicable — or whether the combination of scale, ad revenue, e-commerce profitability, and membership economics is now structural and accumulating.

03AI & Ops
AI · Ops
Mondelez: AI-enabled DCs and a deliberate retreat from "complex state-of-the-art lines"
Supply Chain Dive · Antone Gonsalves · May 19, 2026
EVP/COO/CFO Luca Zaramella told analysts on Mondelez's April 28 earnings call that the company will deploy automation and AI at up to five DCs serving its direct-store-delivery network, covering 55 branches. About 60% of US manufacturing has been modernized; some plants in the remaining 40% run on high waste and "below expectations" productivity. Zaramella: "We have come to terms that some of the plants will have to deal with much simpler lines as opposed to having complex state-of-the-art lines." The work sits inside a $1.2 billion multi-year ERP and supply chain transformation approved July 2024, completion expected year-end 2028.
Why it matters
The operating answer to a 60/40 modernization split is not to over-engineer the laggards — it's to right-size them to what they can actually run well. That is an operational stance with implications for CPG and grocery alike, and it cuts against the prevailing assumption that "more automation" is always the answer.

AI · Ops
Amazon's voice-commerce move resets the AI-shopping bar — but the moat is the shopper graph
Retail Dive · Bryan Wassel · May 14, 2026
Amazon launched Alexa for Shopping on May 13, replacing its 2024 Rufus assistant with a personalized AI shopping experience that operates across the Amazon app, website, and Echo Show, with a "Buy for Me" feature that transacts across non-Amazon online stores. Retail Dive surfaced two named-analyst reads on what the move actually does. Neil Saunders, managing director of GlobalData Retail: "It's not just the fact that it's an AI tool that benefits, it's the fact that Alexa and Amazon know a great deal about individual consumers so can use that information to enhance the experience in a personal way. Few other retailers have this level of data and insight." Brad Jashinsky, director analyst at Gartner: "AI is only one of the methods consumers will use for shopping, and retailers will continue to invest in traditional methods like search."
Why it matters
The competitive question is not whether other retailers can deploy conversational AI but whether they have the shopper-graph depth to make it personal in the way Amazon can. As of the 30-day window ending May 26, no major conventional grocer has publicly named Amazon's voice-commerce move as a competitive trigger. The absence of named reaction is itself signal.

04Board & Governance
Governance
Heidrick: one in three US CEOs lacks the capabilities the next two to three years require
Heidrick & Struggles · May 12, 2026
A survey of 1,033 CEOs and board members globally, including 253 in the US, finds more than a third of US companies have a CEO who lacks the capabilities the organization needs to succeed over the next two to three years. Directors are more concerned than CEOs themselves — 46% report a discrepancy. 93% of respondents expect changes to strategy and operating model in the next two to three years, with half expecting significant change. External hires now account for 27% of Fortune 500 CEO appointments since 2024, up from 18% prior.
Why it matters
Only 25% of leaders treat CEO succession as a priority — and one-third have no specific KPIs for the succession process. Boards expecting strategic transformation without matching investment in the leadership transition that funds it are running a structural risk most have not yet priced.

The dignity argument moves from theology into AI alignment
Notre Dame News · May 25, 2026
Pope Leo XIV released his first encyclical on May 25, addressing AI's effects on work, dignity, and the human person. In the Notre Dame faculty response published the same day, philosopher Meghan Sullivan — whose Institute for Ethics and the Common Good runs on a $50.8 million Lilly Endowment grant, and who attended an Anthropic summit in March on Claude's moral development — framed the encyclical's core argument: "Christian tradition has never grounded human dignity in cognitive performance or economic productivity. It has never said: You matter because of what you can do. It says: You matter because of who you are."
Why it matters
The argument that human worth is not a function of output is moving into the AI-alignment conversation through named academic voices with operational standing in how frontier models are being built. Whether one shares the source tradition or not, the framing — cognitive performance is not what dignity rests on — has implications for how operators justify, design, and explain workforce decisions as AI takes over cognitive and economic work humans previously did.

Culture
Three rationalizations that destroy change leaders — and how AI outputs make each one easier
Fast Company · Amy Radin · May 28, 2026
Columbia lecturer Amy Radin, writing in Fast Company, identifies three pressure points where change leaders drift into rationalization — drawing on Ann Tenbrunsel and David Messick's foundational research on "ethical fading." The Conviction Trap: being right when others are uncertain becomes the credibility that makes legitimate objections sound like resistance. The Coalition Compromise: translating a message for different stakeholders slides into managing what each one knows. The Urgency Override: when genuine time pressure becomes the justification for compressed due diligence and stakeholders not consulted.
Why it matters
Radin's argument: AI outputs provide cover for each trap. The Conviction Trap is easier when you run only the query that confirms what you already believe. The Coalition Compromise smooths when you can show stakeholders a recommendation rather than own a position. The Urgency Override finds justification when the output arrives fast and the decision feels documented.

Oddly Interesting
The next era of sample-return missions is being conducted from Tanegashima and Wenchang
Space.com · Robert Lea · December 31, 2025
Two small-body sample-return missions are operating from Asian launch and operations centers in the back half of 2026, both targeting destinations no spacecraft has visited before. JAXA's Martian Moons eXploration probe (MMX) launches from Tanegashima in November on an H3 rocket, aiming to land on Phobos, collect at least 10 grams of surface material, and return the first-ever sample from the Mars system to Earth in 2031. CNSA's Tianwen-2 — China's first asteroid mission — arrives at the quasi-satellite Kamoʻoalewa this summer to attempt a sample collection from a body that follows an unusual Earth-co-orbiting path, with sample delivery to Earth scheduled before the decade ends.
Why it matters
The two leading sample-return missions of this generation are JAXA-led and CNSA-led. Both extend operational expertise in small-body proximity operations that has commercial implications for future asteroid prospecting and lunar resource work, and both are happening largely outside the US-centric coverage that frames space as a SpaceX-and-NASA story. The structural fact is that planetary-science capability has diffused.
