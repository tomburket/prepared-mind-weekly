Board · Governance
Albertsons named Meg Whitman executive chair, a newly created role, at $100,000 salary and $8.9 million in restricted stock
Grocery Dive · September 9, 2026
Albertsons expanded its board from 10 to 11 directors on Sept. 8, appointed Meg Whitman to the new seat and elected her executive chair the same day; Kim Fennebresque moved from chair to lead independent director. The 8-K sets her pay at an annual base salary of $100,000 plus restricted stock units with a grant date value of $8.9 million, vesting in one year. Because she is an employee, the board determined she is not an independent director, and she sits on no committee. Susan Morris, chief executive officer, said: "At this pivotal moment, I'm thrilled Meg is joining our board as Executive Chair."
Why it matters
An executive chair is a working executive seated above the chief executive, and the role did not exist at Albertsons before Sept. 8. The one-year vest is the horizon the board is paying for.

02AI & Ops
AI · Ops
Instacart launched Clementine, an AI assistant that builds a grocery cart from a request, a recipe or a photo of a list
Instacart · September 9, 2026
Instacart introduced Clementine on Sept. 9, available to most U.S. and Canadian customers in its app and on its website. A typed request, a recipe, a dietary preference or a photo of a handwritten list becomes a cart from the shopper's chosen store. Instacart says Clementine orders carry more items than its typical basket and exceed its $115 average, without giving an order count. Food Bazaar, Heritage Grocers Group and Woodman's are already running Cart Assistant, the same engine under the grocer's own name. This is a new product; the Instacart item in Edition 22 was a single checkout prompt that lifted sales nearly 1 percent.
Why it matters
Edition 22's item was one prompt at checkout. This is the full assistant, and the white-label version is already inside three grocers' own apps, which puts Instacart's AI between those retailers and their customers.

AI · Ops
Google DeepMind released predictions for all 9 billion possible single-letter changes in the human genome
IEEE Spectrum · September 8, 2026
DeepMind published the AlphaGenome Atlas on Sept. 8: precomputed predictions for 9 billion single-nucleotide variants, every single-letter substitution possible in the human reference genome, with about 27,000 predictions per variant across hundreds of cell types. The dataset is one petabyte, which DeepMind says is more than 30 times the size of the AlphaFold Database. It is free for academic use, with commercial access through Google Cloud described as coming soon. The accompanying manuscript is a company technical report with no DOI and no peer review.
Why it matters
Running the model once and storing the answers turns genome-scale variant analysis into a lookup. The scores are only as good as the model, and no one outside the company has reviewed either.

03The Workforce
The Workforce
Aimbridge launched a labor forecasting tool across its hotels and reported productivity gains without a number
Hotel Dive · September 2, 2026
Aimbridge Hospitality, the Plano, Texas, hotel management company, launched LIFT on Sept. 1, a tool that puts labor forecasting, scheduling and daily execution on one screen for property and finance managers. The company said pilot hotels saw measurable labor productivity gains, largest in housekeeping and laundry, and closer alignment between planned and actual labor. It gave no figure for any of it. Keryn McNamara, chief information officer, said LIFT "directly supports owner profitability."
Why it matters
Housekeeping and laundry are the largest schedulable labor pools in a hotel, so that is where a forecasting tool shows first. What Aimbridge has published is a direction, not a size.

Inspire Brands' chief executive is on medical leave and Dunkin' president Scott Murphy will run the company in the interim
Nation's Restaurant News · September 9, 2026
Paul Brown, cofounder and chief executive of Inspire Brands, is on medical leave while he recovers from an injury, The Wall Street Journal reported Sept. 9; the company confirmed it by email to Nation's Restaurant News. Scott Murphy, brand president of Dunkin', will serve as interim chief executive. Inspire, owned by Roark Capital, runs Arby's, Dunkin', Buffalo Wild Wings, Sonic, Jimmy John's and Baskin-Robbins and has been planning an initial public offering this year. No effective date, duration or expected return has been given.
Why it matters
An IPO candidate now has an interim chief executive with no stated term. The company has disclosed less about the change than the newspaper that reported it.

Nestlé will invest CHF 310 million in Brazilian nutrition manufacturing, including a new infant-formula plant
just-food · September 3, 2026
Nestlé said Sept. 3 it will invest CHF 310 million in Brazil by 2028, building an infant-formula factory in Ituiutaba and expanding its Araçatuba hub in São Paulo state. The new plant is expected to open in the second quarter of 2028 with about 100 jobs. Jeff Hamilton, chief executive of Nestlé Zone Americas, said nutrition "accounts for around 20% of Group sales." The announcement came two days after Nestlé agreed to sell its mainstream supplements business for $1 billion.
Why it matters
Within one week Nestlé sold a supplements business and committed capital to infant and medical nutrition. The two moves mark what it treats as core and what it does not.

05Cultural Signal
Culture
OpenAI claims an unrefereed AI-generated proof on the Navier–Stokes problem, one of math's Millennium Prize problems
MIT Technology Review · September 8, 2026
On Sept. 8 OpenAI said an unnamed model, running as roughly 10,000 concurrent agents, produced a proof that a smooth fluid at rest can develop a singularity in finite time, a result it presents as addressing the Navier–Stokes problem, one of the Millennium Prize problems in mathematics. The proof was formalized in the Lean proof assistant but has not been reviewed by a journal. Two mathematicians, Tristan Buckmaster of NYU and Levent Alpöge of Anthropic, had posted a related result on the Euler equations days earlier; OpenAI credits them, and Buckmaster says he is "not accusing anyone of anything." Terence Tao wrote Sept. 3 that AI-produced solutions could inhibit a field's development.
Why it matters
Whether the proof holds is a question for referees, and the company has not submitted it to any. Until it does, the claim rests on OpenAI's word and a formal checker.

06Oddly Interesting
Oddly Interesting
After 21 seasons of digging, Iran's Burnt City yielded a 4,600-year-old board game of a type never seen before
Tehran Times · September 2, 2026
Archaeologists in the 21st excavation season at Shahr-i Sokhta, the Bronze Age Burnt City in southeastern Iran, uncovered a 4,600-year-old board game that Rouhollah Shirazi, who heads the team, called "a previously unknown type." Shirazi said the discovery could offer a new perspective on social life and entertainment at the settlement, which was inhabited through the third millennium BC. Excavation at the site began in 1967. The same season produced an engraved bone comb with no known regional counterpart, along with eggshell, textile and leather remains.
Why it matters
Twenty-one seasons and nearly sixty years in, the site is still producing objects its own excavators have never seen. The game's rules are unknown, and the dating is the team's, with laboratory work in progress.

A Louisville food distributor won a DLA fresh produce contract worth up to $497 million over five years
U.S. Department of Defense · September 8, 2026
The Defense Logistics Agency awarded Creation Gardens Inc., doing business as What Chefs Want, of Louisville, Kentucky, a maximum $496,849,673 indefinite-delivery contract for fresh fruits and vegetables on Sept. 8. The five-year contract serves Army, Air Force and Department of Agriculture customers in Illinois and runs to Sept. 7, 2031. The acquisition was competitive and three offers were received.
Why it matters
The announcement gives a ceiling, a term and a bidder count, and nothing about volume or price. Three companies judged five years of produce for three federal customers in one state worth bidding on.
