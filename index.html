Board · Governance
Clorox hired Unilever’s Fabian Garcia as CEO on a $100,000 salary, with the rest of the package paid in stock
Clorox 8-K · October 29, 2026
Clorox’s board appointed Fabian Garcia, 66, a Unilever business group president, chief executive effective Jan. 5, 2027, replacing Linda Rendle, who steps down as CEO and chair and advises the company through April 2 at her current $1,350,000 salary, an 8-K filed Oct. 29 says. His offer letter sets a $100,000 base, a $2,000,000 target bonus paid in shares, an $11,000,000 sign-on grant and a $9,100,000 annual equity target from September 2027. Because the salary is nominal, Clorox will size his severance off a “peer median salary,” initially $1,250,000.
Why it matters
A board brought in an outside CEO at a $100,000 salary and then had to invent a reference salary so its own severance math would still work.

Red Robin’s two investor-designated directors resigned and waived replacement seats, and the board will shrink from eight to six
Red Robin 8-K · October 28, 2026
James Pappas, managing member of JCP Investment Management, and Chris Martin, managing director of Jumana Capital, resigned from Red Robin’s board and all committees Oct. 23, the company said in an 8-K filed Oct. 28; both were seated under a December 2024 cooperation agreement. Their firms waived the right to name replacements, and the board intends to go from eight directors to six, five of them independent. JCP holds about 10.3% of the stock and Jumana about 9.3%, per their Oct. 27 13D amendment, and the exit follows a $115 million JPMorgan-led refinancing.
Why it matters
Activists seated in a 2024 settlement step off once the refinancing closes and keep roughly a fifth of the stock between them.

The case for Anthropic’s S-1: revenue up fourteenfold, two dollars per compute dollar and 250 customers paying $10 million a year
Anthropic S-1 · October 26, 2026
Anthropic’s S-1, filed Oct. 26 to list on Nasdaq as “ANT,” reports $16.3 billion of revenue for the first half of 2026 against $1.2 billion a year earlier, and Fortune’s Jeremy Kahn calculates the company took in more than two dollars of revenue for every dollar it spent on compute. The filing counts more than 11,900 customers spending at least $100,000 a year, more than 1,900 at $1 million and roughly 250 at $10 million, CNBC’s Ashley Capoot reported Oct. 27. NYU’s Aswath Damodaran values the equity at $1.475 trillion, “at the lower end of the offer pricing range.”
Why it matters
The bulls’ case is that the buyers are already there, 250 of them at Fortune 500 procurement scale, and that the unit economics turned before the IPO rather than after it.

The case against Anthropic’s S-1: fixed costs, customers who can leave in an afternoon and a board the public can’t touch
Los Angeles Times · October 28, 2026
The S-1 Anthropic filed Oct. 26 commits it to about $265.6 billion in fixed compute spending while its customers, five of whom supply about a quarter of revenue, can cut back at will, the Los Angeles Times’ Michael Hiltzik wrote Oct. 28. Maryland’s Siva Viswanathan, in commentary distributed by his business school, put the switching risk in a line: “Switching model providers can take an afternoon, not the years often required to migrate cloud infrastructure.” NYU’s Aswath Damodaran wrote that a trust electing the board majority and a founder share carrying 50.1% of the vote “effectively emasculates public investors.”
Why it matters
The bears’ case is structural, not cyclical: the costs are locked, the customers are not, and the shareholders being asked to fund it cannot change the board.

Chipotle posted a third straight quarter of transaction growth, with comparable sales up 2.1% and restaurant margin down to 23.3%
Chipotle · October 28, 2026
Chipotle’s comparable restaurant sales rose 2.1% in the third quarter, made up of a 1.3% increase in average check and a 0.8% increase in transactions, the third consecutive quarter of positive transaction growth, per its Oct. 28 release. Restaurant-level margin (non-GAAP) fell to 23.3% from 24.5%; labor rose to 25.7% of revenue from 25.2% and other operating costs to 16.0% from 15.0%, while food, beverage and packaging fell to 29.5% from 30.0%. Revenue grew 9.6% to $3.3 billion, and 53 of 84 new restaurants included a Chipotlane.
Why it matters
Chipotle is adding customers again, three quarters running, while labor and other operating costs take the margin back.

Kroger set a 2029 comp target of about 3% and swapped its shareholder-return goal for an earnings-growth goal
Kroger investor release · October 20, 2026
Kroger told investors on Oct. 20, nine days before this edition, that it targets roughly 3% comparable sales growth excluding fuel and pharmacy in 2029, 3% to 6% adjusted EBITDA growth and 7% to 11% adjusted EPS growth a year through 2029, excluding the pending Giant Eagle acquisition, its own release says. The EPS target “replaces the previous 8%-11% total shareholder return target, which included the dividend yield, and implies a stronger earnings outlook.” CEO Greg Foran called the plan “making value more visible and focusing resources closer to the customer where we see the highest returns.”
Why it matters
Kroger’s new three-year frame measures earnings alone, strips out the dividend and the pending acquisition, and names the savings that are supposed to pay for the customer investment.

USDA forecast food-at-home prices up 2.3% in 2026 and 1.0% in 2027, with a 2027 range from -5.2% to 7.5%
USDA Economic Research Service · October 23, 2026
USDA’s Economic Research Service forecast Oct. 23 that all food prices will rise 2.8% in 2026, with food-at-home prices up 2.3% and food-away-from-home prices up 3.5%. For 2027 it forecasts food at home up 1.0%, inside a 95% forecast interval of -5.2% to 7.5%, and all food up 1.7%. Beef and veal lead 2026 categories at 8.7%, and eggs are forecast to fall 29.3%; in September, food prices were 2.5% above a year earlier, against 3.7% for all items.
Why it matters
The government’s own 2027 grocery forecast is close to flat, and its range runs from a 5% drop to a 7.5% rise.

AI · Ops
Walmart wrote the rules for AI agents that shop for its customers: separate profiles, spending caps and “we price products, not people”
Walmart corporate news · October 21, 2026
Walmart said in an Oct. 21 post on its own corporate site, eight days before this edition, that it will introduce Agent Profiles “in the next month,” letting a customer authorize each AI agent separately and cap it at a limit such as “$50, $100 or $200,” which the post compares to “adding someone as a cardholder on your credit card.” A Walmart Agent Gateway, opening as a developer beta, will let agents shop through what the post calls “an MCP (model text protocol)” rather than pages built for people. Its terms state that “agents that do not follow these terms will be blocked.”
Why it matters
Walmart has published, in its own words, the permission structure for a shopper who is software, down to the spending limit and the promise that the agent sees the same price a person does.

The space station’s seventh roll-out solar array unfurled itself Oct. 29, mounted in front of the old wings
NASA · October 29, 2026
NASA’s Anil Menon and the Canadian Space Agency’s Joshua Kutryk installed the seventh roll-out solar array on the International Space Station’s 2A power channel Oct. 29 in a 7-hour, 11-minute spacewalk, NASA’s station blog reported; the eighth goes up Nov. 5, raising station power from 160 kilowatts to about 215. Each array arrives rolled like a snap bracelet and opens on its own on energy stored in carbon-fiber booms, with its cables connected only in Earth’s shadow, Spaceflight Now’s Houston Harwood reported Oct. 28. Rather than replace the original wings, NASA mounted the new arrays in front of them so the unshaded portions keep producing.
Why it matters
The upgrade that will power the station to its 2030 deorbit was done by putting new arrays in front of the old ones, not by taking the old ones down.

Milwaukee won its first World Series on a bottom-half payroll, and its owner credited succession planning, not spending
ESPN · October 28, 2026
The Brewers beat Tampa Bay in five games, closing it out Oct. 28 at Tropicana Field for the franchise’s first title, after a record 103 wins and an 11-2 postseason, ESPN’s Bradford Doolittle reported. It was the first World Series since 1991 between two bottom-half payrolls, Milwaukee 19th and the Rays 25th, five weeks before the labor agreement expires Dec. 1 with owners proposing a $245.3 million cap, AP’s Ronald Blum reported. Owner Mark Attanasio: “If you study businesses and organizations, you want to have good succession planning. Before you even get to Matt [Arnold] and Pat Murphy, you had David Stearns and Craig Counsell.”
Why it matters
Baseball’s smallest market won on the 19th-ranked payroll and the owner’s explanation was continuity in the front office, while the commissioner says the same result argues for a cap.
