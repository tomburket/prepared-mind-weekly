Hy-Vee is reworking pharmacy, prepared food and online pickup at stores around Des Moines
Supermarket News · September 18, 2026
Hy-Vee remodeled a West Des Moines, Iowa, store, relocating its pharmacy and expanding prepared foods, and moved online pickup in nearby Waukee from the parking lot into the store, Supermarket News reported Sept. 18. The grocer plans to test a drive-through pharmacy in Waukee, Grimes and Ankeny and to convert its Dollar Fresh Market banner to the full Hy-Vee format with service meat and in-store bakeries. “What we’re really looking at is, ‘How do we continue to move to where the customer wants us to go long term?’” CEO Jeremy Gosch said.
Why it matters
The changes pull pharmacy, online pickup and prepared food closer together inside the building.

Every Target store team member completed training this year on three service standards: easy, inspiring and friendly
Target · September 16, 2026
All Target store team members completed additional training this year on three service standards, the company said Sept. 16, covering more than 350,000 people in more than 2,000 stores. Each standard has a floor-level shorthand: “Zoned, in stock, clean and fast”; “Visual presentation and interactions”; and “Greet, help, thank.” Target said it has invested “hundreds of millions of dollars this year in additional payroll” and that guest satisfaction metrics reached three-year highs in the first quarter.
Why it matters
More than 350,000 people now share one three-part definition of a good store visit, backed by added payroll.

Teamsters at a Kemps dairy plant in Minnesota ratified a contract that raises the night shift differential 400 percent
International Brotherhood of Teamsters · September 17, 2026
More than 110 members of Teamsters Local 471 ratified a two-year contract at Kemps’ dairy plant in Farmington, Minn., the union announced Sept. 17; Kemps is a Dairy Farmers of America subsidiary. The deal includes unspecified wage improvements, a 2 percent cut in the health care cap, a 2 percent increase in pension contributions and a 400 percent increase in the night shift differential. The union says more than 70 percent of the bargaining unit benefits from the night shift increase.
Why it matters
The contract’s largest single change is to night shift pay, which the union says reaches most of the unit.

A new geophysical survey points to sealed chambers beyond the walls of Tutankhamun’s tomb
Nature · September 17, 2026
A team led by former Egyptian antiquities minister Mamdouh Eldamaty published radar and microgravity data Sept. 17 that it says points to a “hidden complex” of corridors and rooms beyond the walls of Tutankhamun’s tomb, Nature reported. The theory, first proposed in 2015, is that the unexpectedly small tomb conceals further chambers that may hold Nefertiti; outside geophysicists called the new data intriguing but not conclusive. If Egypt’s Supreme Council of Antiquities approves, drilling to send in camera-equipped miniature tractors could begin in November.
Why it matters
After a decade of conflicting surveys, the question now rests on a permit to drill a few meters and look.

Gravity pulling on Earth’s inner core mainly drives the decades-long, millisecond-scale swings in the length of a day
Nature · September 23, 2026
Earth’s day lengthens and shortens by a few milliseconds over decades, and a Nature paper published Sept. 23 by Huifeng Zhang and Mathieu Dumberry of the University of Alberta finds the main driver is gravity between dense regions of the inner core and the mantle. The outer core’s flow pulls the inner core out of alignment, and gravity slowly pulls it back, a contest resisted by electromagnetic and topographic forces at the core’s edge. “Before we obtained the result, we didn’t know they are competing with each other,” Zhang told Nature.
Why it matters
The study assigns the drift in the length of the day to one dominant force, where earlier work had proposed several.

Oddly Interesting
Researchers burned their own papyrus scroll to test a way to find readable Herculaneum scrolls before scanning them
PLOS ONE · September 16, 2026
In a PLOS ONE paper published Sept. 16, researchers from UC Berkeley, NIST and two British institutions wrote on papyrus with lead-spiked carbon ink and then carbonized the rolled scroll in a low-oxygen furnace. X-ray tomography and virtual unrolling made the words “the children of” legible without enhancement, while lines in plain carbon ink did not show; a handheld analyzer of the kind used to find lead in paint detected lead on the burned scroll. The authors call it “the first confirmed proof of concept” that lead ink would greatly improve the odds of reading Herculaneum’s scrolls.
Why it matters
The team did not read a real Herculaneum scroll; it tested a cheap screen for deciding which fragile scrolls are worth expensive imaging.
